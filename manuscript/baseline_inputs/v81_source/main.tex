\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Background.} Regional myocardial dysfunction after infarction is spatially
heterogeneous, and global measures such as ejection fraction describe net chamber
emptying, not where and when deformation is lost. Routine balanced steady-state
free-precession cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) is acquired in almost every clinical
examination and is now the usual input for automated motion and strain estimation. Unlike tagging or
displacement encoding with stimulated echoes, it does not deliberately encode dense material
correspondence, so motion is estimated under assumptions rather than observed.

\textbf{Methods.} A critical narrative review with a structured search of PubMed/Europe PMC, OpenAlex,
Crossref and arXiv, plus citation expansion and official dataset, repository and regulatory records,
to a 1 September 2026 cutoff with one documented post-cutoff update. Screening was by one person,
without independent duplication. One question is asked: to what extent does current evidence
support the claim that motion and strain from routine cine CMR recover the magnitude, location,
extent and timing of regional dysfunction, and where does direct validation remain absent?

\textbf{Findings.} Conventional registration, unsupervised learning-based registration,
cardiac-specific estimators, physics-informed representations and reference-supervised networks are
compared on what each is trained to do and how each has been validated. Across these methods
the most frequently reported evidence is anatomical overlap, image similarity or sparse
landmark error; agreement with a material-sensitive reference is less common, and evaluation on
prescribed focal abnormality rarer still. Where regional comparisons exist, agreement is
consistently weaker than global agreement: in one 61-participant comparison, intraclass correlation
coefficients against tagging were 0.88--0.94 for global longitudinal and circumferential strain
against 0.44--0.77 regionally, with radial values of 0.05--0.51, the least consistent
component. Studies that infer infarction from cine alone validate against tissue references, which
answer a different question.

\textbf{Conclusions.} Evidence for routine cine CMR is uneven rather than uniformly absent: several
global measurements are well characterised, whereas direct regional evidence is confined to a few
conditions and is mixed rather than uniformly discouraging. This
review sets out what a study must declare for a regional attribute to be called
conditionally recoverable, and identifies the designs that would close the gap, without presupposing
that any method family fails.
\end{abstract}
