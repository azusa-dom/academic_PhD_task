\section{Why regional function is a distinct measurement problem}

Regional strain from routine cine CMR is already reported in clinical research. Paired comparisons with tagging and DENSE support global and mid-ventricular circumferential strain under matched definitions, and the same pipelines are now asked a harder question: the magnitude, location, extent and timing of a focal abnormality, such as the scar after infarction. Among the studies reviewed here, direct tests of those four attributes at the lesion remain sparse. The available evidence answers other questions: slice-averaged error in a simulated scar, segment-level agreement with DENSE, discrimination of scarred segments against late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), scan--rescan precision and prognosis. Read as answers to one question, these results appear to contradict each other. Understanding why requires separating what cine observes from what a regional report claims, and that separation is the subject of this review.

Left-ventricular (LV) ejection fraction compresses a complex three-dimensional deformation into a chamber-level ratio. That compression has real value: ventricular volumes and ejection fraction are reproducible CMR endpoints, widely understood and embedded in clinical reporting standards~\cite{schulzmenger2020,hundley2022}. The same compression also hides information. Well-functioning regions can compensate for a smaller abnormal region, opposing changes can cancel in an average, and the timing of regional contraction can change without a large fall in ejection fraction. Regional motion and strain are attractive precisely in the clinical settings where such hidden changes matter: focal infarction, heterogeneous cardiomyopathy, dyssynchrony, early dysfunction and treatment response.

Clinical studies support the relevance of measuring deformation in those settings. Feature-tracking strain has been associated with functional recovery after myocardial infarction, adverse remodelling and cardiovascular outcomes~\cite{buss2015,reindl2021,chadalavada2024}, and DENSE circumferential strain has shown prognostic association after acute infarction~\cite{mangion2019}. These associations motivate evaluating deformation. They leave open how accurately a regional abnormality has been located or quantified, because a prognostic marker can remain useful despite measurement bias, and a technically accurate motion field may still fail to improve a clinical decision. Analytical validity, biological association and clinical utility are linked questions with separate answers~\cite{raunig2015,puntmann2018}.

Earlier reviews have covered the analytical-validity question at the level of strain physics, modality differences, clinical practice and sources of discrepancy~\cite{amzulescu2019,smiseth2025}, and more recent reviews cover deep learning for wall-motion analysis across modalities and strain from CMR and computed tomography~\cite{monfared2026review,hussein2026review}. The present review addresses a narrower question within that literature. It asks how faithfully routine cine CMR can recover \emph{regional or focal} myocardial dysfunction, and how the choice of validation target changes the claim that can be made. Answering that question requires comparing estimator families by their inputs, assumptions, estimands, populations and reference standards: how each family uses image information, representation, priors and supervision to infer motion and strain, and which spatial and temporal scales of measurement its validation evidence supports. Within that comparison, the focal case is the most demanding one, and it is used here as a test of the evidence base, without treating every method paper as if it had set out to solve it.

The practical question is:

\begin{quote}
For a specified cine acquisition, estimator and measurement definition, which attributes of a regional abnormality (magnitude, location, spatial extent and timing) are recovered within a tolerance that is appropriate for the intended use?
\end{quote}

This formulation steers between two unhelpful extremes. At one extreme, routine cine would be treated as information-free; in reality myocardial borders, papillary muscles, trabeculation, intensity variation, neighbouring structures and temporal consistency all constrain motion. At the other extreme, a visually plausible deformation or an accurate contour would be treated as proof of correspondence; in reality neither uniquely identifies how material points moved within the myocardium. Between these extremes, the evidence supports bounded successes, and the review is organised to state those bounds.

The review proceeds in three steps. \Cref{sec:families} compares the estimator families by what each adds to the images: inputs, representation, priors and supervision. \Cref{sec:validation} and \cref{sec:reconcile} sort the validation evidence by the question each target can answer (\cref{tab:evidence} and \cref{tab:attributes}) and show why results that appear contradictory are often answers to different questions. \Cref{sec:programme} then sets out the tests that a regional or focal claim requires, with a reporting template in Supplementary Note~5.

\subsection{Review approach and scope}

This critical narrative review combines selected method papers, empirical comparisons, reviews and public-resource descriptions; it does not follow a systematic-review protocol and does not attempt a census of cine-strain studies. Sources came from citation expansion around a core set and from a structured search of PubMed, Europe PMC and OpenAlex run on 3 September 2026 with a publication cutoff of 1 September 2026, whose six query families covered regional strain after infarction, known-motion validation, prior perturbation, conditional recoverability, failure-aware prediction and regional localisation or attenuation. A single software-assisted title-and-abstract screening pass, a directed check on 9 September, an October update that added sources published after the cutoff, and a recheck of central quantitative claims against primary-source methods, results and tables on 11 October 2026 followed. The original record exports and record-level screening decisions are not available for re-audit, so this account describes the search without reproducing it; query counts, dates and their limits are given in the Supplementary Methods.

Reading depth varies by source. Central comparisons were checked in the relevant full-text sections where accessible; many method-lineage entries rest on abstracts or bibliographic records and support only what is available at that depth; the evidence tables mark the difference. Missing detail in an abstract is not evidence that a study omitted an experiment, and the review identifies priorities for focal validation without claiming that any design is absent from the entire literature. The studies in \cref{sec:families} to \cref{sec:programme} were compared along six axes: image input and sampling; population and abnormality; the estimated entity, such as contour motion or dense displacement; strain definition and aggregation; validation target; and reported error or association. Because these axes, reference standards and endpoints were too heterogeneous to share a scale, results were not pooled, and Dice overlap, displacement error, strain bias, scan--rescan precision, scar discrimination and outcome association were kept as different quantities throughout.

\section{From cine images to a regional measurement}

\subsection{What routine cine CMR observes}

Balanced steady-state free-precession (bSSFP) cine CMR samples a time series of image intensities. In a typical short-axis image, the blood--myocardium interfaces are conspicuous, while intramyocardial texture can be weak and changes with coil sensitivity, through-plane motion, partial volume, reconstruction and artefact. The image therefore supplies anatomical and intensity cues without a persistent identity label for every tissue element. Every estimator has to add something to those cues. Feature tracking exploits image features or propagated contours; registration exploits an image-similarity relation; biomechanical models constrain admissible deformation; and learning-based methods import associations learned from training data~\cite{pedrizzetti2016,duchateau2020}. These four categories overlap in practice: a neural network may predict registration parameters, a registration method may use a learned similarity measure, and a biomechanical penalty may be embedded in either.

The separation between observed cues and inferred correspondence in \cref{fig:image-to-report}a is a causal one, and it can be written down. Let a point in a reference configuration be \(\vect X\), and let an estimated mapping be \(\boldsymbol\varphi(\vect X,t)\). The displacement and deformation gradient are
\begin{equation}
\vect u(\vect X,t)=\boldsymbol\varphi(\vect X,t)-\vect X,
\qquad
\vect F(\vect X,t)=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\varphi}{\partial\vect X}
=\vect I+\frac{\partial\vect u}{\partial\vect X}.
\label{eq:kinematics}
\end{equation}
One possible finite-strain measure is the Green--Lagrange tensor,
\begin{equation}
\vect E=\frac{1}{2}(\vect F^{\mathsf T}\vect F-\vect I).
\label{eq:gl}
\end{equation}
These equations (\cref{eq:kinematics} and \cref{eq:gl}) state how a chosen mapping is converted into a chosen strain; they say nothing about whether \(\boldsymbol\varphi\) is correct. The conversion itself has consequences for error. Differentiation can amplify local mapping error, while smoothing can reduce variance and alter peaks or spatial gradients. The reported number further depends on coordinate axes, myocardial layer, reference frame, segment definition, spatial aggregation and whether ``peak'' means the minimum of a segment-average curve or the average of pointwise minima. A comparison that ignores these choices can attribute a definitional difference to a tracking error~\cite{voigt2015,wehner2018}.

\subsection{The same contours can support different material mappings}

Boundary agreement constrains deformation without determining it. Consider an annulus whose end-diastolic and end-systolic contours are fixed. Material points can retain their angular positions, or they can be reparameterised around the same final contour. Both mappings give Dice overlap of one and Hausdorff distance of zero for the masks, while their circumferential stretch varies differently with position (\cref{fig:same-contours}). The counterexample proves a limited point: equal masks or equal mean geometry are insufficient to identify intramyocardial correspondence. It leaves two other questions open, namely whether cine-derived regional strain is wrong in any particular case, and whether image texture and temporal information can select a useful mapping.

The consequence of this non-uniqueness grows as the claim becomes more local. Chamber volume can be estimated from boundaries alone. A global contour-length change is also strongly boundary-constrained. A segmental average additionally requires correspondence between sectors. Transmural or voxelwise strain requires, on top of that, intramyocardial correspondence and spatial derivatives. Along this sequence, a method can be accurate for volumes and useful for global strain while remaining insufficiently validated for focal strain gradients. Validation should therefore follow the finest claimed spatial and temporal scale; credibility earned at a coarser scale does not transfer downward.

\subsection{A complete estimand is more than the word ``strain''}

Because each step of that sequence adds a definitional choice, a regional strain estimand should state at minimum:

\begin{itemize}
\item the reference configuration and mapping direction;
\item the finite-strain measure (for example, linear, Green--Lagrange or logarithmic strain);
\item the anatomical coordinate system and sign convention;
\item the myocardial layer and dimensional model (2D slice, 2D+time or 3D+time);
\item the differentiation, interpolation and spatial regularisation used;
\item the region definition, registration to the American Heart Association (AHA) segment model and treatment of junctions;
\item the temporal summary, including end-systolic versus peak strain and handling of post-systolic shortening; and
\item the order of spatial and temporal aggregation.
\end{itemize}

When these items are missing, apparent disagreement may reflect different quantities. The problem is most prominent for radial strain, for which wall-thickening surrogates, tensor components and layer-specific measurements are not interchangeable. It also affects circumferential comparisons whenever one pipeline reports one-dimensional engineering strain and another reports a component of a two-dimensional Green--Lagrange tensor~\cite{wehner2018,cao2018}.

\subsection{An underdetermined problem, and what the prior is doing}\label{sec:illposed}

Let \(I_t(\vect x)\) denote image intensity at position \(\vect x\) in phase \(t\), and let \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{0\rightarrow t}\) map a material point in the reference configuration to its position at \(t\). A representative intensity-based registration estimator solves the objective
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\boldsymbol\varphi}_{0\rightarrow t}
=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\varphi_{0\rightarrow t}}
\mathcal D\!\left(I_0,\,I_t\circ\boldsymbol\varphi_{0\rightarrow t}\right)
+\alpha\,\mathcal R(\boldsymbol\varphi_{0\rightarrow t}),
\label{eq:registration}
\end{equation}
in which \(\mathcal D\) rewards image agreement, \(\mathcal R\) encodes prior structure and \(\alpha\) sets their balance. The map is written here as a forward map on the reference domain. Frameworks that optimise a backward sampling map use a different convention, and the two conventions must not be interchanged when transformations are composed or differentiated for strain. Groupwise registration extends such an objective over a sequence, while an amortised registration network can minimise its expectation during training. Other families use different targets: StrainNet learns from DENSE-derived displacement, DLSS uses velocity and segmentation supervision, and a downstream disease model learns from clinical labels. Their training losses, inference mappings and evaluation references must therefore be distinguished. No universal image-matching loss unites these methods. What they share is the need to combine observed information with representational and learned assumptions.

Where intramyocardial texture is weak, different fields may achieve nearly the same image agreement while implying different displacement gradients. The prior then helps select the estimated field; \cref{fig:same-contours} demonstrates the narrower case in which only masks constrain correspondence. Three factors require separate tests. Input information limits what can be identified from the images. Representation limits which fields the model can express. Estimation determines which admissible field the optimiser or network recovers. These factors can interact: a bandwidth restriction, for example, changes both representational capacity and optimisation, and displacement agreement does not guarantee agreement of its spatial derivatives.

A direct fit to a known field probes the representation under a stated norm, sampling scheme and boundary treatment, with displacement and the target strain or abnormality attribute evaluated separately. Running the estimator on rendered images probes recovery under the combined image and estimation conditions. Changing regularisation, resolution or views can expose sensitivity, but causal attribution needs matched controls and retraining when a training-loss weight changes. These experiments diagnose possible mechanisms; they do not assign errors to mutually exclusive causes. The interpretation of representation fitting is developed in \cref{sec:threequestions}, and the proposed programme specifies the additional optimisation checks needed before a failed fit can establish a capacity limit.

Temporal strategy adds a further assumption to the objective. Reference-to-frame tracking keeps one reference configuration and must therefore estimate large displacements from it; sequential tracking estimates smaller inter-frame steps and accumulates composition error and drift; groupwise methods estimate all phases jointly under whatever temporal constraint they impose~\cite{metz2011,chen2024}. None of these strategies guarantees correct intermediate phases, because a periodic deformation can close perfectly at the end of the cycle and still be wrong in between. Cycle closure is therefore a useful constraint and a useful quality-control signal, and no more than that.

\subsection{Feature tracking and contour propagation}\label{sec:ft}

Feature-tracking (FT) software is attractive because it operates on routine cine and fits existing clinical workflows. Initial contours are propagated through the cardiac cycle, after which strain may be calculated from endocardial length, a myocardial mesh or a vendor-specific deformation field; interior motion is to a large degree interpolated from boundary behaviour~\cite{pedrizzetti2016}. Because each of these steps can be implemented differently, the phrase ``feature tracking'' denotes a family of measurements. Commercial implementations have differed in contour placement, transmural treatment, radial-strain calculation, temporal interpolation and user correction. As a consequence, values may fail to be interchangeable across products or software versions even when repeatability within a product is acceptable~\cite{lim2021,militaru2021}.

The family's clinical validation began before deep learning. Maret and colleagues related cine-derived functional measurements to scar in 30 patients after ST-elevation infarction~\cite{maret2009ft}; Hor and colleagues compared mid-ventricular whole-slice peak circumferential strain with HARP in 191 patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy and 42 controls~\cite{hor2010ft}; and Augustine and colleagues studied 145 healthy volunteers with a tagging comparison in 20, finding closer global circumferential than longitudinal or radial agreement~\cite{augustine2013ft}. These are abstract-level readings, and the three studies validated three different regional quantities. Deep learning entered the family through its front end. Vigneault and colleagues segmented the sequence with a fully convolutional network and fitted quadratic basis splines across all frames in 42 patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and 21 controls~\cite{vigneault2017}, and Puyol-Ant\'on and colleagues combined convolutional segmentation with subsequent feature tracking and strain calculation~\cite{puyolanton2018}. In both designs the learned component is the segmentation, and the motion model remains a conventional tracker.

The strongest interpretation of a contour-tracking result depends on what was actually tracked. A reliable change in endocardial length can support a global or segment-level contour-derived strain under that definition; it cannot by itself support transmural material strain. The converse caution also applies: a method that uses internal image information should be described by its documented algorithm, and a product-category label such as ``border tracking'' should not replace that description. Method descriptions and validation endpoints, then, should govern interpretation. One evaluation caveat applies across \cref{sec:families}. Segmentation can initialise the search region, define the cardiac coordinate system and, in several learning-based designs, supply an anatomical loss during training. When the same reference contours both shape the fitted transformation and provide the overlap score that assesses it, the evaluation is not independent of the fitting.

\subsection{Conventional and learned registration, and the field representation}\label{sec:registration}

Intensity-based deformable registration estimates a dense field by optimising \cref{eq:registration} directly. The free-form deformation model of Rueckert and colleagues, an affine transform plus a B-spline control lattice with a smoothness penalty, is a widely used representation originating in breast MRI~\cite{rueckert1999}. Groupwise variants remove the dependence on an arbitrarily chosen reference frame. Metz and colleagues fitted an \(n\)D\(+t\) B-spline model to the whole dynamic sequence with spatial and temporal smoothness and an optional cyclic constraint, reporting sub-voxel accuracy on synthetic and thoracic CT data~\cite{metz2011}. More recently, Chen and colleagues replaced the groupwise dissimilarity term with a locally low-rank metric and evaluated it on a 20-case simulation, 100 public cine cases and 16 in-house tagging patients. Both groupwise metrics reduced late-diastolic drift relative to Farneback optical flow and pairwise registration, and the locally low-rank version correlated better with tagging for radial strain in several segments than those two methods and than the global low-rank version. \(n\)D\(+t\) B-splines and pTVreg were compared on simulated tracking and strain errors and on public-data contour tracking; they were absent from the patient tagging comparison~\cite{chen2024}. Optical flow itself offers another route to local correspondence: Wang and colleagues combined intensity and shape-feature invariance in a gradient-based method for cine and tagged MR, evaluated on realistic simulations and preliminary clinical data~\cite{wang2019opticalflow}. Two further designs supply correspondence through geometry or mechanics: a Laplace-based mapping between successive contours~\cite{elnakib2015laplace} and variational assimilation of cine observations into a contraction model~\cite{tuyisenge2016assimilation}. The Laplace method was tested on synthetic data with in-vivo feasibility. For variational assimilation, the original abstract reports known-motion synthetic tests and 47 patients imaged at basal, mid-ventricular and apical levels, radial and circumferential strain agreement with tag tracking (intraclass correlation coefficient, ICC, above 0.8), and associations with clinical wall-motion scores. These positive findings remain abstract-level evidence here; the inferred trajectories in both approaches depend on their modelling assumptions. Anatomy-aware, pyramidal and online-adaptive methods change the problem along the same lines, by altering the evidence available to the optimiser and the class of admissible fields~\cite{chen2020anatomy,yu2020mpn,yu2020foal}.

The learning-based literature began by amortising \cref{eq:registration}. VoxelMorph reformulated registration as a function, parameterised by a convolutional network, that maps an image pair to a deformation field; the unsupervised training objective is the same image-matching and smoothness objective that a classical method optimises per pair, and the gain is that inference becomes a single forward pass~\cite{balakrishnan2019voxelmorph}. TransMorph replaced the convolutional encoder with a hybrid Transformer--convolutional architecture and added diffeomorphic and Bayesian variants, validated on brain MRI and phantom-to-CT registration~\cite{chen2022transmorph}. In this family the prior is still smoothness or diffeomorphism, now partly learned, and the reported evidence is label overlap and deformation-field regularity.

A distinct strand places the constraint in the representation itself. B-splines, finite-element bases, Fourier coefficients, implicit neural representations and dense voxel grids have different capacity and conditioning. Fourier-Net predicts a band-limited representation of the displacement in a low-dimensional frequency domain and recovers the full-resolution field by zero-padding and an inverse discrete Fourier transform~\cite{jia2023fouriernet}; Fourier-Net+ takes band-limited representations of the images themselves as input, with cascaded and diffeomorphic variants~\cite{jia2026fouriernetplus}. Bandwidth explicitly constrains the base representation, making it a useful variable for the attribution experiment in \cref{sec:illposed}. It does not necessarily constrain the final displacement to the same spectral support: Fourier-Net reports that scaling-and-squaring in its diffeomorphic variant produces a field that is no longer band-limited, and composition in cascaded variants also requires separate assessment. The implications for focal strain therefore depend on the complete mapping; the retained coefficients alone do not determine them. Both networks were developed as general registration methods. Fourier-Net+'s published abstract reports an evaluation on five datasets, including one 3D cardiac MRI dataset on which its diffeomorphic variant outperformed TransMorph and other baselines in Dice, Hausdorff distance, end-diastolic volume and ejection fraction. These are registration, geometric and functional measures; they do not by themselves establish regional material-strain fidelity. Direct evaluation of a band-limited cine estimator against a regional motion or strain reference is therefore a priority for the comparison proposed here, and the incomplete historical search record does not support any broader claim that such an evaluation is absent.

\subsection{Cardiac-specific designs: joint tasks, multiple views, meshes and time}\label{sec:cardiacspecific}

A second group builds cardiac structure into the estimator. Qin and colleagues learned motion estimation and segmentation jointly, sharing a multi-scale encoder between an unsupervised Siamese recurrent spatial-transformer branch and a segmentation branch, in a total cohort of 220 subjects, including 100 held-out test subjects~\cite{qin2018joint}. Zheng and colleagues learned a pixel-wise ``apparent flow'' between two cine phases semi-supervised from a large unsegmented and a small expert-segmented set, then combined motion features derived from segmental radius and thickness with shape, volume and ejection-fraction features. The nine-feature classifier achieved 95\% accuracy in five-fold cross-validation on the Automated Cardiac Diagnosis Challenge (ACDC) training cohort and 94\% on the held-out challenge test set~\cite{zheng2019apparent}. In that study the motion representation is validated through its usefulness for a downstream label, a pattern that recurs in \cref{sec:supervised}. Motion Pyramid Networks (MPN) introduced multi-scale fused estimation with cyclic teacher--student training. In addition to mask comparisons, it measured endpoint and keypoint-tracking errors after applying motion generated by a separate network to images and initial keypoints; this is known imposed motion from a model; it does not supply an independent physiological reference~\cite{yu2020mpn}. FOAL added online adaptation of a learned estimator to a new sequence at test time~\cite{yu2020foal}. A 2025 study used a memory-based model over all cine frames and evaluated supervision choices in 271 patients. Its abstract reported EPEs of 1.02~mm for the base model, 0.95~mm with end-diastolic distance-map supervision and 0.91~mm with full supervision, compared with 1.19~mm for a retrained RAFT baseline. Its evaluation points were manually initialised on the first-frame contours and propagated by conventional feature tracking; they were not motion-training targets. The reported EPE therefore measures agreement with those tracked points. Global peak-strain correlations provide a separate endpoint~\cite{portal2025semisup}.

Multi-view and mesh-based designs target through-plane motion. MulViMotion fuses short-axis and long-axis cine in a hybrid 2D/3D network with a shape-regularisation module providing weak supervision, using 580 UK Biobank subjects split into 450 for training, 50 for validation and 80 for testing~\cite{meng2022mulvimotion}. DeepMesh represents the heart as a 3D mesh of endocardial and epicardial surfaces, propagates a template mesh into subject space and estimates vertex-wise displacement through a differentiable mesh-to-image rasteriser, maintaining temporal vertex correspondence and common template indexing across subjects; that indexing does not establish material homology~\cite{meng2024deepmesh}. CardioMorphNet takes the shape idea further: a recurrent variational autoencoder with separate posteriors for segmentation and motion, trained by recursively registering segmentation maps without a warped-image intensity-similarity term (its variational objective retains an image-reconstruction loss), validated on UK Biobank and M\&Ms by comparing warped masks with reference masks and producing voxel-wise uncertainty maps~\cite{movahed2026cardiomorphnet}. Predictive uncertainty and accurate warped masks are valuable outputs, with two limits: the uncertainty is not automatically a calibrated regional error, and a Bayesian output is not automatically an abstention policy. STCMT-Net represents cardiac motion through keypoints extracted without supervision and reconstructs the dense field as a weighted combination of basis vectors from matched keypoint pairs, evaluated on 4D CT and cine MRI with a strain analysis reported to separate patients under different conditions~\cite{qiao2026stcmt}.

Temporal structure has been addressed in parallel. Bidirectional recurrent registration predicts reference-to-frame motion with sequence context~\cite{lu2023bireg}; SegMorph couples motion and segmentation in a recurrent variational representation~\cite{bi2025segmorph}; and IG-BiReg combines a bidirectional residual fusion network with global constraints in mean-intensity and principal-component feature spaces; its published abstract reports mask and contour agreement on ACDC and M\&Ms, and material-strain validation was not verified~\cite{jia2026igbireg}. A further variant is a Mamba-based sequential model listed in the programme of a 2025 MICCAI workshop~\cite{yin2025mcm}. A continuous-time latent neural ordinary differential equation adds bidirectional forward--backward evolution and a Lagrangian consistency term, evaluated on ACDC and M\&Ms~\cite{ruan2026latentode}. One parallel development belongs here because of what it leaves out. CMR-specific foundation models now exist, one pre-trained on 15 million cine images from 74,916 studies~\cite{fu2026cinema} and another trained by self-distillation on 36 million images from 27,524 subjects~\cite{jacob2025cmrfm}, each fine-tuned for segmentation, landmark localisation, diagnosis and related tasks. The retrieved task descriptions do not include dense motion estimation. Their segmentation and clinical-task results establish useful representations, while motion-specific transfer remains outside the evidence inspected here.

Together, these studies have made progress on three-dimensional motion from two-dimensional acquisitions and on temporal consistency across the cycle. Their reported validation needs stating plainly. In the cardiac-specific papers reviewed here, the dominant evidence is agreement between a warped segmentation and a reference segmentation, together with surface distances and deformation-field regularity, with landmark error where a landmarked dataset was available. That evidence is appropriate for the claims those papers make, and it is also the evidence that \cref{fig:same-contours} shows cannot by itself establish material correspondence.

\subsection{Biomechanical and physics-informed regularisation}\label{sec:physics}

A third group replaces a generic smoothness penalty with a statement about tissue mechanics or about the function space itself. Qin and colleagues learned the prior from data: a variational autoencoder trained on biomechanically simulated deformations defines a manifold of plausible fields, and tracking becomes a search over that manifold, validated on three public cine datasets~\cite{qin2023biomech}. WarpPINN states the physics explicitly. It represents the deformation with a neural network fitted per case under an intensity-similarity term plus a hyperelastic regulariser and a Jacobian penalty for near-incompressibility. Strain follows by automatic differentiation, and Fourier feature mappings allow sharp features in the strain field. It was tested on synthetic examples and a cine benchmark of 15 healthy volunteers~\cite{arratia2023warppinn}. WarpPINN-fibers adds a fibre-stretch penalty and tests displacement recovery against prescribed motion in a synthetic phantom. On the same healthy benchmark it reports improved landmark tracking and compares estimated strain curves for physiological plausibility; no reference strain curves were available to establish improved human strain accuracy~\cite{alvarez2026warppinn}. Liu and colleagues combine the mechanical and temporal arguments in a space--time finite-element correlation framework with region-specific biomechanical regularisation and a data-driven temporal decomposition. The published abstract reports evaluation against two feature-tracking and four deep-learning baselines using one synthetic dataset with known motion and strain, three public datasets with landmarks or masks and one clinical dataset with masks. The known-motion tests concern 2D\(+t\); 3D\(+t\) motion was evaluated only on the clinical dataset using masks~\cite{liu2026fe}.

Such results show that mechanics can be useful inductive structure. They do not imply that a constrained field is uniquely correct, because disease can alter wall thickness, loading, scar mechanics and fibre organisation, and a 2D slice omits through-plane motion. The studies summarised here combine controlled synthetic tests with in-vivo landmark or mask evaluation. These targets establish different properties, and the available summaries do not resolve focal strain recovery across pathological conditions. A mechanical prior is a differently shaped constraint from a smoothness prior, neither weaker nor stronger in general, and whether it preserves a focal deficit depends on whether its constitutive assumptions hold in heterogeneous injured tissue. That dependence is an open question, and validation must test it directly by asking whether the constraint preserves the abnormality of interest, in addition to assessing plausibility.

\subsection{Learning from cine, contours or motion-encoded CMR}\label{sec:supervised}

The following examples distinguish the acquisition used at inference, the learning target and the endpoint used for evaluation. They include cine pipelines and methods that analyse the material-sensitive comparator itself. DeepTag estimates motion on tagged images without labels, using bidirectional diffeomorphic registration between consecutive frames and a differentiable composition layer to recover the Lagrangian field, validated by landmark tracking on a clinical tagging dataset~\cite{ye2021deeptag}. Networks trained on synthetic 3D tagged images from a biophysical left-ventricular model have since been evaluated for 3D tagging analysis, including an external human landmark comparison and porcine strain comparisons with manual cine annotations~\cite{buoso2025tagging3d}; like DeepTag, they operate on the comparator acquisition and not on cine. DeepStrain provides an end-to-end cine workflow. It centres the ventricle, segments anatomy, estimates end-diastole-to-phase displacement and derives Green--Lagrange radial and circumferential strain, and the original study reported a Cardiac Motion Analysis Challenge (CMAC) landmark end-point error of \(2.9\pm1.5\)~mm together with scan--rescan repeatability in ten healthy volunteers and high agreement for volumetric measures~\cite{morales2021}. A later single-centre comparison in 47 healthy participants and 533 patients found correlations with manual feature-tracking analysis of 0.83--0.85 for global radial and 0.88--0.91 for global circumferential strain~\cite{morales2022comparison}; in that comparison the comparator is another estimator. These results establish workflow and output properties. Independent dense material truth for the cine motion field is a separate matter, and a prescribed-motion evaluation of DeepStrain is discussed in \cref{sec:knownmotion}.

StrainNet is particularly relevant because DENSE phase-derived displacement was the supervised target, and its two evaluations must be read separately. Of 305 eligible individuals (161 patients, 99 healthy adults and 45 healthy children or adolescents), 243 individuals contributing 670 sections formed the training set and 62 individuals contributing 190 sections formed the participant-held-out internal test set; the 305 were not independent held-out cine tests. For pixelwise displacement end-point error (EPE), the model was deliberately given contours derived from DENSE magnitude images, permitting one-to-one comparison with DENSE phase-derived displacement over the myocardium and time series; the held-out-test EPE was \(0.75\pm0.35\)~mm. In the distinct routine-cine analysis, contours from bSSFP cine feature tracking were supplied to StrainNet and registered DENSE was the comparator. End-systolic global and AHA-segmental circumferential Lagrangian strain, derived from the estimated displacement (the exact tensor implementation sits in a technical appendix that was not accessible), was analysed in 59 of the 62 test participants with basal, mid-ventricular and apical images: ICCs were 0.87 versus 0.72 globally and 0.75 versus 0.48 segmentally for StrainNet versus conventional feature tracking~\cite{wang2023strainnet}. The improvement in ICC was accompanied by prose-reported cine--DENSE limits of agreement of \(-5.74\) to 4.72 percentage points globally and \(-9.16\) to 8.07 percentage points segmentally. A small discrepancy between the source prose and table for the global limits is documented in Supplementary Table~S1. The study used an internal participant split, with cine-mask preprocessing and resolution matching; external-dataset validation was not performed. These results support improved end-systolic Ecc agreement, while the remaining individual differences require comparison with an intended-use tolerance. The two tests retain distinct interpretations: the EPE is not a cine-contour result, ``global'' strain is not the pixelwise whole-myocardium EPE, and end-systolic values are not peak-strain timing. The inference therefore remains model-, direction-, population-, acquisition- and registration-specific, and it does not establish focal location or extent.

The same supervision route has since been extended. Lei and colleagues describe a DENSE-supervised model with separate myocardial-rotation and radial-motion branches for routine bSSFP cine; the retrieved abstract motivates this design by difficulty recovering large rotation~\cite{lei2025denserotation}. MyoNet takes a related approach in Dahl salt-sensitive rats with radiation-induced injury. Cine segmentations are converted to binary spatiotemporal inputs, and displacement supervision is obtained by applying sine-wave modelling (SinMod) to paired tagged MRI. Reported circumferential and radial strain ICCs were 0.973 and 0.975~\cite{an2026myonet}. This is evidence for a segmentation-to-motion learning route in a preclinical setting, with an analysis-derived tagging reference; it does not establish accuracy in human routine cine.

Clinical labels enter these pipelines at different stages. Cine-only infarct delineation combines recurrent motion features and optical flow with manual delayed-enhancement delineations as the target~\cite{xu2018}. Deep-learning synthetic strain (DLSS) predicts myocardial velocities and segmentation, using velocity supervision from registered 4D-flow MRI and segmentation targets generated by commercial software, and derives strain from cine at inference. Its development dataset contained 223 examinations from 219 patients; four-reader consensus wall-motion labels were used for the separate clinical evaluation in 53 patients and 846 segments. The ROC AUC was 0.90; a 30\% peak-radial-strain threshold gave sensitivity 86\% and specificity 85\%~\cite{masutani2023dlss}. Thus the reader labels evaluate the clinical output; they do not supervise the motion network.

Deep-learning aligned strain also separates motion estimation from clinical classification. Its cine pipeline detects key phases, segments the myocardium and estimates deformation using image similarity, smoothness and mask agreement; LGE labels the downstream patient-level fibrosis classifier, and the displacement field is learned without LGE. Development used 57 patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy, followed by a same-centre temporal holdout of 82 patients. The classification target was the presence of at least one LGE-positive segment; the spatial boundary of fibrosis was not a target~\cite{koehler2025alignedstrain}. In a different chamber and population, Bello and colleagues used right-ventricular motion to predict survival in 302 patients with chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension. Internally bootstrap-corrected Harrell C indices were 0.75 for the learned model and 0.59 for a model using manually measured conventional volumetric and functional variables~\cite{bello2019}. Both studies provide clinically relevant discrimination evidence in their tested settings, without converting that evidence into dense motion accuracy or demonstrated treatment benefit.



\section{What the validation literature establishes}\label{sec:validation}

No single study can answer every layer of the measurement chain, so the most informative synthesis groups evidence by validation target; algorithm chronology, the organising axis of \cref{sec:families}, is set aside here. \Cref{fig:evaluation}a summarises what each target can establish. The following subsections take the targets in turn, from the most controlled to the most clinical.

\subsection{Known-motion tests establish capacity and technical error under controlled conditions}\label{sec:knownmotion}

Numerical phantoms make exact correspondence available. A simple realistic phantom showed that spatial and temporal resolution can materially affect feature-tracking measurements~\cite{adams2023}. MRXCAT2.0 couples statistical shape, biophysical simulation and tissue texture to produce healthy and pathological cine-like images paired with known functional ground truth~\cite{mrxcat2023}. Other in-silico heart models and DENSE-SIM extend controlled testing of strain pipelines and acquisition effects~\cite{mukherjee2024,barbaroux2025}. The design that such resources make possible, a graded regional deficit measured against a material reference while the estimator is held fixed, has a precedent outside cine: Yeon and colleagues compared tagged circumferential shortening with implanted sonomicrometry crystals in a canine coronary-ligation model, with both methods separating ischaemic from remote myocardium~\cite{yeon2001sonomicrometry}. This provides a precedent for regional reference-based testing, although it is not a controlled sweep of lesion size or severity in routine cine.

MRXCAT2.0 provides a useful prescribed-focal example: an evaluation of DeepStrain by a separate development team. Four left-ventricular cases were generated (normal, dilated, hypertrophic and infarcted; ejection fractions 51\%, 34\%, 41\% and 49\%). An elliptical free-wall scar was prescribed in the infarct case, while remote compensation preserved its ejection fraction. The underlying abnormality differed by component: remote-to-scar peak systolic strain changed from 0.95 to 0.30 radially (Err), from \(-0.18\) to 0.01 circumferentially (Ecc) and from \(-0.18\) to \(-0.17\) longitudinally (Ell). Radial strain was markedly reduced but retained a substantial residual value; circumferential strain approached zero, while longitudinal strain changed little. These are prescribed tissue values; the estimator's errors are reported separately below~\cite{mrxcat2023}.

DeepStrain achieved a reported myocardial Dice of 0.82 across phases and displacement error of \(1.0\pm0.9\)~mm. The reported circumferential strain error was \(0.02\pm0.04\), compared with radial strain error of \(-0.24\pm0.21\) across cases and \(-0.20\pm0.21\) in the infarct case. The source's Figure~14 averages the measures over three mid-ventricular short-axis slices; these summaries do not quantify error within the scar. Although the source describes the infarct case as the lowest performer, with its lowest Dice in the thin scar region, the infarct-case radial mean error is smaller in magnitude than the all-case mean. Neither these means nor overlapping standard deviations establish a uniform case ranking or equivalence. Different metrics can rank cases differently, and the all-case summary includes the infarct case. \Cref{fig:attributes-mrxcat} preserves the reported values while separating them from unmeasured scar attributes.

The generated domain also limits interpretation. The evaluation used one scar geometry and a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 30, and myocardial texture was removed during image generation. The MRXCAT2.0 texture network (TextNet) and DeepStrain were both developed with ACDC data: independence of the evaluating team does not establish independence of the image domain, although shared dataset use alone is not evidence of leakage. The inspected source does not identify the evaluated DeepStrain release or weights. The component-specific errors could reflect image formation, wall thinning and partial volume, training data or estimation constraints; the experiment does not isolate these contributions~\cite{mrxcat2023}.

Known-motion resources allow investigators to test representation capacity, recovery at a chosen signal-to-noise ratio and sensitivity to sampling or regularisation. Existing work has varied resolution~\cite{adams2023}, varied analysis regularisation~\cite{barbaroux2025}, or represented regional pathology in simulated motion~\cite{mrxcat2023,zhou2018straus}. These are useful precedents for the proposed validation programme. The surviving search records do not establish whether all combinations of graded lesion size, severity, transmurality and fixed-estimator evaluation have already been studied. Here, graded attribute recovery is proposed as a research priority; the proposal does not rest on a claim of field-wide absence. Simulation results remain conditional on the anatomy, mechanics, image formation and pathology represented; paired in-vivo comparisons are needed to assess transfer.

\subsection{Paired tagging or DENSE provides material-sensitive in-vivo evidence}

Tagging introduces a prepared magnetisation pattern; DENSE encodes displacement in image phase. Both make myocardial motion more directly observable than routine cine, and neither is error-free. Tag fading, spatial filtering, phase unwrapping, signal-to-noise, through-plane motion, temporal fitting and registration to cine can all contribute reference error. A comparison should therefore report reference uncertainty and acquisition alignment, and it should avoid calling either technique literal ground truth.

The paired literature is mixed in a scientifically useful way. In three healthy volunteers and 41 patients with recent or chronic myocardial infarction, Gao and colleagues reported broadly similar end-systolic regional circumferential strain between cine registration and DENSE, while cine radial strain was overestimated, particularly in healthy and non-infarcted myocardium~\cite{gao2014cine}. A larger comparison of 77 patients with cardiomyopathy and ten controls found relatively small circumferential differences but wide radial differences across tagging, DENSE and three feature-tracking products~\cite{cao2018}. Wehner and colleagues showed that part of the apparent cine--DENSE discrepancy can arise from comparing different strain definitions, while still finding that regional tracking information cannot be inferred from contours alone~\cite{wehner2018}.

The product dependence seen by Cao and colleagues was examined directly at 3~T by Militaru and colleagues, who compared three feature-tracking products with tagging in 61 participants. Global longitudinal and circumferential agreement was high, regional and radial agreement varied more, and scar discrimination differed by vendor~\cite{militaru2021}. This pattern supports global and some regional use under specified products, and it warns against interchangeability and against extrapolating global performance to radial or highly local outputs; it does not justify a blanket rejection of feature tracking. Among the paired studies reviewed here, timing was compared less often than magnitude. Among 42 enrolled participants, 30 contributed to the circumferential tagging--feature-tracking comparison, in which Brandt and colleagues found ICCs of 0.82 for global and 0.74 for septal and lateral time to peak strain, alongside significant biases in segmental peak strain~\cite{brandt2024}. This supports a regional timing comparison at the septal and lateral level; the study did not establish lesion-scale timing accuracy, and ICCs of this size do not establish individual interchangeability.

A 2026 study makes the importance of the endpoint explicit. Jahromi and colleagues compared one- and two-slice DENSE models with a one-slice cine model in a deforming phantom and 40 healthy volunteers. In the volunteer comparison, the endpoint was slice-wise peak median systolic endocardial circumferential strain at two mid-ventricular levels. The cine versus two-slice DENSE mean difference was \(-2.7\) percentage points. Two one-sided tests supported equivalence within a selected margin of \(\pm3.5\) percentage points, despite a significant difference from zero; those are compatible findings because they test different hypotheses. Prospective specification of the margin and the handling of within-person slice correlation were not established by the inspected report. The radial, longitudinal and epicardial circumferential comparisons differed~\cite{jahromi2026densecine}. Thus the result supports a specific healthy-volunteer, slice-level, endocardial comparison; it says nothing about focal-lesion accuracy or general interchangeability. Selected studies are compared in \cref{tab:evidence}.

\subsection{Repeatability establishes precision, and leaves accuracy open}

Scan--rescan studies are essential for longitudinal use, because they show how much a result may change when the underlying subject is assumed stable. The Strain-8 study scanned 20 healthy volunteers twice on the same day using eight protocols: cine, tagging and DENSE at 1.5 and 3~T, plus 2D and 3D echocardiography. Global repeatability ranged from fair to excellent depending on direction, protocol and field strength, while segmental results were more variable~\cite{bell2025strain8}. A multicentre travelling-volunteer study found relatively good inter-scanner agreement for global longitudinal and circumferential strain, lower agreement for global radial strain and larger software-related differences~\cite{goetze2026}. Two cautions follow from such studies. A repeatable biased method can still be inaccurate, and a method with good global repeatability can have weaker segmental precision. Both absolute error and variance should therefore be reported at the claimed scale.

\subsection{Tissue references test biological correspondence, and leave motion truth open}

LGE is an independently measured tissue characteristic with strong relevance to infarction, and it is not a displacement measurement. If regional strain differs between scarred and non-scarred segments, the result supports tissue association and possibly diagnostic discrimination. It leaves the pointwise displacement error unknown, because scar transmurality and deformation are related without being identical, and remote loading, tethering and compensation can alter strain.

Kihlberg and colleagues provide a clear example. In 116 patients with a high likelihood of coronary artery disease, 34 had more than 50\% transmural LGE in at least one segment. For discriminating those segments, reported areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUCs) were 0.87 for DENSE, 0.83 for tagging and 0.66 for feature tracking; at 80\% specificity, sensitivities were 82\%, 71\% and 35\%, respectively~\cite{kihlberg2020clinical}. This is clinically relevant comparative evidence that the techniques captured scar-associated dysfunction differently in that cohort. Read as a direct ranking of material-motion accuracy, it would say more than it measured.

Buss and colleagues associated global feature-tracking strain with patient-level recovery of left-ventricular ejection fraction at six months after infarction~\cite{buss2015}. He and colleagues evaluated segmental-strain measures for prognosis after ST-elevation infarction~\cite{he2024}. Such findings strengthen biological and clinical qualification. They also illustrate why motion accuracy and clinical usefulness should both be studied: a regionally smoothed estimate might retain prognostic signal, and a physically accurate field may fail to improve a decision if the endpoint or treatment pathway is poorly specified.

\subsection{Outcome association is downstream evidence}

Global or regional strain has been associated with adverse events in disease cohorts and in a population-based cohort~\cite{mangion2019,chadalavada2024}. Such associations establish prognostic value under the model, covariates and follow-up that were studied. Whether changing management on the basis of the measurement improves outcomes is a further question, and answering it requires a decision context, a comparator, a threshold, the consequences of error and preferably an impact evaluation. The distinction matters most for a regional map, where multiple testing, correlated segments and post hoc threshold selection can produce apparently focal findings.

\section{Reconciling apparently contradictory findings}\label{sec:reconcile}

The literature looks contradictory when all endpoints are treated as interchangeable. Once differences in input, estimand and target are made explicit, the same literature becomes more coherent, and the following four subsections examine the main sources of apparent contradiction.

\subsection{Global agreement and regional disagreement can coexist}

Spatial averaging suppresses zero-mean local errors. If one segment is overestimated and another underestimated, a global mean may remain accurate. Boundary-constrained deformation can also reproduce total shortening while distributing it differently around the wall. For both reasons, strong global correlation and small global bias do not mathematically imply correct location or extent of a regional abnormality. Regional validation must therefore evaluate regional errors directly and account for correlated repeated segments within participants.

The reverse is also possible. A method may preserve the location of a focal reduction while showing a scale bias in absolute strain. Whether that output is useful depends on the intended task: localisation, longitudinal change, diagnostic threshold or mechanistic interpretation. A single correlation coefficient conceals these possibilities. Studies should report bias, limits of agreement and participant-level distributions for quantitative strain; localisation, extent and timing errors are additionally needed when those attributes form part of the intended claim.

\subsection{Radial strain is a special stress test}

Radial strain is repeatedly less consistent than circumferential or longitudinal strain~\cite{cao2018,militaru2021}. Three features of the measurement explain much of this. The ventricular wall spans relatively few voxels radially, trabeculation complicates the endocardial boundary, and a small contour displacement can produce a large relative change in thickness. Products may additionally calculate radial output from different entities. These factors make the definition and uncertainty of radial strain unusually important, without making radial function unknowable. Claims should therefore specify whether the result is tensor radial strain, wall thickening, a layer-to-layer distance change or another surrogate.

\subsection{Regularisation can help and can bias}\label{sec:threequestions}

Smoothness, temporal bases, incompressibility and learned priors stabilise an ill-conditioned estimate. They can reduce noise and prevent folding, and they may also spread or attenuate a focal abnormality if the imposed scale is too coarse. Which of these effects dominates is an empirical matter: a smooth representation with sufficient basis functions can represent a focal defect, and an unconstrained dense field can still miss it because image cues are weak or optimisation fails. Three questions should therefore be separated:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Representation capacity:} can any parameter setting express the target motion at the required scale?
\item \textbf{Estimation:} given images that contain sufficient cues, does the optimiser or trained model recover those parameters?
\item \textbf{Input sufficiency:} do routine cine images contain enough information, under the tested acquisition and pathology, to distinguish the relevant motions?
\end{enumerate}

Each question needs its own test. A representation-capacity test should not be replaced by a registration score, and failure on clinical cine should not automatically be attributed to the basis. In the other direction, success on noiseless synthetic images does not establish that the clinical input contains discriminating information. \Cref{sec:illposed} describes controlled comparisons that probe these potentially coupled contributions, and the DeepStrain evaluation on MRXCAT2.0 in \cref{sec:knownmotion} shows why it is needed: the same estimator on the same images preserved one strain component better than another at the slice-average level. Nothing in that single experiment says which of the three was responsible.

\subsection{Uncertainty and quality control answer a different question}

An uncertainty map can describe posterior dispersion, ensemble variability or sensitivity to perturbation. It becomes a clinically interpretable error estimate only after calibration against relevant reference errors. Quality control stands in the same relation to accuracy: it can detect image artefact, contour failure or field folding, and it cannot guarantee that an accepted case has accurate regional strain. Methods should therefore report the observable signal used by a quality rule, its coverage, and error among accepted and rejected cases. A selective or abstaining workflow is appropriate only when such a rule is actually implemented, and it should not be imposed as a rhetorical requirement on every estimator. The consolidated mapping from validation target to supported claim is provided in Supplementary Note~2.

\section{A proposed validation programme for regional or focal claims}\label{sec:programme}

A proposed evaluation programme should combine controlled tests, material-sensitive comparisons and clinical endpoints around an explicit measurement contract. Its objective is to match the strength of the claim to the evidence provided, which does not require every possible experiment from every paper. \Cref{fig:evaluation}b shows the resulting evaluation flow, and the subsections below describe its stages.

\subsection{Pre-specify the measurement contract}

Before comparing methods, investigators should state the cine protocol, views, temporal and spatial resolution, reconstruction, image-quality exclusions, abnormality type and scale, estimator version, user interaction, strain definition, reference alignment, target attributes and tolerances. Case splits should occur at participant and, where relevant, centre level. Repeated segments should not be analysed as independent observations. Hyperparameters, model selection and any quality threshold should be chosen without using the final test set. Competing methods should be tuned on the same data with comparable effort, because a retrained baseline and a published score answer different questions; and releasing the transformation direction, differentiation code and strain definition makes a disagreement diagnosable where a single aggregate score does not.

The primary endpoint should be an error in the claimed attribute, even when a surrogate is easier to obtain. If the claim is detection of a focal region, suitable endpoints include centre-of-abnormality distance, overlap or surface distance of the abnormal region, with sensitivity across lesion sizes. If the claim is quantitative severity, bias and limits of agreement for regional magnitude are required. If timing matters, peak-time or curve-alignment error should be assessed in addition to peak amplitude.

\subsection{Stage 1: challenge representation and sampling with known motion}

Known-motion experiments should vary one factor at a time and then test interactions: abnormality width, transmural extent, amplitude, onset/peak timing, position, wall thickness, through-plane motion, temporal resolution, spatial resolution, contrast, noise and artefact. The known field should first be fitted in the candidate representation under an explicit norm, sampling scheme and boundary treatment. The fitting objective must match the target: minimising displacement error need not minimise strain-gradient or focal-attribute error. A failed numerical fit alone does not establish a representation limit. Such a conclusion requires an analytic restriction, a certified approximation bound, or convincing evidence that the best attainable fit was reached; otherwise optimisation remains an alternative explanation. Both displacement error and error in the chosen strain or abnormality attribute should be reported. Running the estimator on rendered images then measures recovery under the combined image and estimation conditions; the difference between these tests is informative but is not automatically an additive or uniquely attributable optimisation error.

An equal-mean counterexample is particularly useful at this stage (\cref{fig:evaluation}c). Two fields can have the same global strain while one contains a focal abnormality. Recovery of their mean is then no evidence of success; the method must distinguish their location and extent within pre-specified tolerances. Such tests show whether apparent global success masks spatial redistribution.

\subsection{Stage 2: use paired material-sensitive in-vivo acquisitions}

The material-sensitive comparison should pair routine cine with tagging or DENSE in the same session and align acquisitions carefully. Reference processing should be blinded to cine outputs where feasible. Registration between acquisitions, slice mismatch and temporal alignment should be quantified. Analyses should report results separately for healthy myocardium and relevant disease patterns, including scar location, wall thickness and image quality. External centres and scanners test transfer only if participants and acquisition conditions are genuinely independent of training and model selection.

No material-sensitive reference is perfect, so this stage also needs a sensitivity analysis. Varying reference smoothing, registration and segmentation estimates how much apparent cine error is attributable to the comparator. Where tagging and DENSE disagree, the disagreement should be reported; choosing the more favourable reference would hide it.

\subsection{Stage 3: connect motion fidelity to tissue and decisions}

Tissue characterisation, expert wall-motion scores and outcomes become valuable after the technical claim is clear. LGE can test whether a regional measurement captures scar-associated dysfunction; perfusion or mapping can address other substrates. Longitudinal studies can test responsiveness and minimal detectable change. Decision studies should specify what action changes, how false-positive and false-negative regional calls matter, and whether the regional measurement adds information beyond volumes, LGE and clinical covariates.



\subsection{Validation resources answer different parts of the question}\label{sec:resources}

The resources reviewed here serve complementary purposes, and none supplies an error-free dense in-vivo reference across broad pathology. STRAUS supplies controlled multimodal images and framewise reference meshes; the Stanford repository supplies human acquisitions with closely matched cine, DENSE and tagging locations; CMAC supplies a historical cross-method benchmark with sparse material-sensitive landmarks; and MRXCAT generations supply programmable motion and acquisition conditions. For each, provenance and the unit of independence matter as much as file availability (\cref{tab:resources}).

STRAUS contains 18 \emph{virtual patients}. These arise from three volunteer-derived multimodal templates, each driven through one healthy and five pathological electromechanical states (left bundle branch block, left-anterior-descending, left-circumflex and two right-coronary-artery ischaemia patterns), so the 18 cases share three anatomies. Each virtual case provides 3D cine MR, three tagged-MR orientations, ultrasound and framewise meshes; the paper used engineering strain for its regional curves~\cite{zhou2018straus,strausdata}. The meshes therefore support known-correspondence displacement and strain calculations when coordinate system, reference frame, mesh identity and temporal sampling are matched. What they cannot supply is the full distribution of human anatomy, disease, image artefact or model error.

The Stanford Cardiac Performance MRI resource has a complementary strength, human acquisitions. Its laboratory page describes 51 healthy volunteers scanned at 3~T from 2023 to 2025, with whole-LV bSSFP cine and DENSE, tagging and mapping at specified corresponding locations~\cite{stanforddata}. DataCite metadata updated on 27 July 2026 describes 55 participants (the same 51 healthy volunteers plus four people with aortic stenosis) under DOI 10.25740/xt223hz2438. The two counts are versioned descriptions of one repository. Neither total proves that every sequence is complete and usable for every participant, that all cine--DENSE slices are registered analysis pairs, or that a derived paper has an independent test cohort. The DENSE magnitude and phase DICOMs also require reconstruction, phase unwrapping, displacement modelling, segmentation, spatial/temporal alignment and an explicit strain definition before they become a reference, so they are material-sensitive observations that become labels only after that processing. Jahromi and colleagues explicitly identify their healthy-volunteer data as part of this repository; individual subject-file correspondence was not verified. No subject files were downloaded or processed for this review.

CMAC paired cine SSFP, 3D tagged MRI and 3D ultrasound from a dynamic phantom and 15 healthy volunteers. Its human reference comprised 12 landmarks (four walls at three LV levels) manually tracked by two observers in 3D tagging, with reported median inter-observer distances of 0.77~mm in the phantom and 0.84~mm in the volunteers~\cite{tobongomez2013cmac,cmac2011}. That reference supports correspondence error at those landmarks and times, and it says nothing about dense strain accuracy between them. The MRXCAT family differs again. Original MRXCAT uses the XCAT phantom to generate cine or perfusion CMR with selectable cardiac/respiratory motion, sampling, coils and noise, and the 2014 demonstration was primarily an acquisition/reconstruction simulator~\cite{wissmann2014mrxcat}. MRXCAT2.0 adds statistical LV shape, biophysical healthy/infarct/dilated/hypertrophic function and generated tissue texture, exposing known simulated function for method testing~\cite{mrxcat2023,mrxcat2repo}. Both are generators with exemplar outputs, so neither has a fixed number of independent participants.

Large multi-centre resources such as CMRxRecon2025 remain useful for reconstruction robustness and disease representation. Its official description covers multiple centres, scanners, disease groups and CMR contrasts, and it does not establish tagging/DENSE motion labels or a fully paired cine--LGE cohort~\cite{cmrxrecon2025official}. The same limit applies to segmentation collections and the Data Science Bowl: containing cine images does not make a collection a regional-motion reference. Detailed access, pairing, scale and uncertainty fields are provided in Supplementary Table~S2 and the editable resource table.

\subsection{Reconstruction evidence is relevant and endpoint-specific}

Reconstruction changes the images supplied to a downstream estimator, and primary studies show both agreement and residual bias. REGAIN was trained on 1616 patients and tested prospectively in 181 participants; its paired breath-hold comparison used 94 patients and 36 healthy participants, while global radial, circumferential and longitudinal feature-tracking strain was available in 49 participants. Mean global-strain differences versus a separately acquired GRAPPA cine were near zero, while the 95\% limits of agreement remained \(-7\) to 6 percentage points for radial, \(-2\) to 3 for circumferential and \(-3\) to 3 for longitudinal strain~\cite{yoon2023regain}. CineVN compared prospectively accelerated acquisitions with conventional GRAPPA in 18 healthy volunteers. For slice-level peak radial and circumferential strain, CineVN had the smallest bias and narrowest limits of agreement among the tested reconstructions, while all accelerated protocols significantly underestimated peak radial strain and overestimated (gave less negative) peak circumferential strain; its 46-patient demonstration lacked a conventional reference and reported slice exclusions after segmentation failure~\cite{vornehm2024cinevn}. These are measured reconstruction-plus-analysis results. Whether deep learning removes or preserves a focal abnormality is a separate question that they do not address.

Other designs bound the inference differently. In ten healthy volunteers, model-based deep-learning reconstruction reduced the dispersion of global circumferential strain differences from the fully sampled comparator relative to compressed sensing at reduction factors 6 and 8, while circumferential bias and substantial radial underestimation remained and no regional lesion was tested~\cite{tsuneta2025dlr}. In 19 participants (13 patients with coronary artery disease and six healthy controls) with 51 compressed-sensing acquisitions, retrospective temporal interpolation changed peak longitudinal and circumferential strain while ventricular volumes and ejection fraction stayed similar~\cite{grob2025interp}; stable volumes do not guarantee stable strain. DENT was developed and evaluated with retrospective cine from 5840 patients across nine centres, with the data of six centres reserved for external testing, and compared interpolated with acquired cine phases, including prospective comparisons in 49 patients and 12 healthy participants. It reported image-domain interpolation error and reader assessment; myocardial strain and focal-abnormality recovery were outside its evaluation, and even its deformation vectors were not validated as motion estimates~\cite{morales2024dent}. Reconstruction and motion estimation should therefore be evaluated jointly when the claim is regional, and any proposed mechanism (temporal smoothing, sharpening, alias suppression or hallucination) remains a hypothesis until tested against an attribute-appropriate reference.

\section{Reporting and interpretation in practice}

The consolidated reporting template is provided in Supplementary Note~5. Its essential logic is to identify the acquisition and reconstruction, estimator/version and interaction, complete strain estimand, statistical unit, validation target, failure policy and tested domain in the same record. For a focal claim, magnitude, location, extent and timing should be reported when each is relevant to the intended use, and an attribute that was not established here should keep that label.

The same record serves two audiences. For clinical readers, a regional strain map should be read together with the cine images, contours, tissue characterisation and global function. For technical readers, improving Dice, smoothness or Jacobian regularity is worthwhile, and the improvement should be connected to the regional quantity that motivates the method. For both, a negative result should examine possible contributions from insufficient input cues, inadequate representation, failed estimation, mismatched strain definition, reference uncertainty or lack of clinical association.

Software status needs the same discipline. Software clearance and commercial availability do not substitute for analytical validation of a specific regional output, and the existence of reference ranges does not make values interchangeable across products. Serial assessment is safest when acquisition, software, version and analysis conventions remain stable, and any change should be bridged empirically.

\section{Limitations of the evidence and of this review}

The evidence base is heterogeneous. Many studies are single-centre or use modest samples, healthy volunteers or a restricted disease spectrum. Paired DENSE/tagging studies face acquisition burden and alignment error. Commercial algorithms are not always described sufficiently to reproduce the exact mapping. Studies often report global or segmental peaks without the full field, and regional analyses may treat segments as independent. Publication bias may favour successful methods and well-behaved images.

This review has corresponding limits. It is a targeted narrative synthesis without duplicate screening, formal risk-of-bias scoring or pooled effects. Original exports, individual screening decisions and citation-expansion rounds from the historical search were not available for re-audit, so its counts and stopping rule remain inherited reports. October additions are separate from the September cutoff. Some recent method entries were inspected only at abstract or metadata depth; their summaries cannot establish that an unmentioned experiment was absent. The updated ledger distinguishes current primary-source checks, inherited details and unresolved access, and the literature map is not a denominator for field-wide prevalence. Product and regulatory details are outside the central synthesis because versions and indications change over time.

The proposed attribute-based framework is an organising approach; it is neither an established regulatory standard nor a new validated biomarker property. Tolerances must be justified for each intended use. No review can determine from published aggregate results whether a particular patient's regional map is correct.

\section{Conclusions}

Routine cine CMR supports useful regional measurements, provided the claim is tied to the measured quantity and validation setting. Paired studies offer the clearest support for selected circumferential-strain endpoints at whole-slice, global and AHA-segment scales. StrainNet improved end-systolic cine--DENSE agreement over its tested conventional feature-tracking pipeline, while residual limits of agreement and internal validation constrain the inference. Repeatability, scar discrimination and prognosis contribute separate evidence about measurement precision and clinical relevance.

A claim about a focal abnormality requires evidence at that scale. DeepStrain on MRXCAT2.0 illustrates the distinction: component-specific whole-slice errors were reported in a prescribed-scar setting, but those averages do not quantify recovery inside the scar. Neither a lower mean circumferential error nor a clinically informative label establishes accurate lesion magnitude, boundary or timing. Apparent disagreement across studies can reflect software, sampling, strain convention, statistical unit and reference uncertainty, as well as estimation error; controlled comparisons are needed before attributing it to information loss or regularisation.

The practical requirement is a measurement contract linking acquisition, estimator, population, spatial unit, strain definition, time point, comparator and tolerance. Known-motion tests can probe representation and recovery; paired cine--DENSE or tagging comparisons can assess in-vivo agreement; tissue and outcome studies can test clinical relevance. Combining these complementary designs with participant-aware statistics offers a way to determine which abnormality attributes routine cine preserves and which remain unresolved, without asking a single validation target to establish them all.
