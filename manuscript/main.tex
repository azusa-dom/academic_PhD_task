\begin{abstract}
Regional myocardial dysfunction can be clinically important despite preserved global ventricular function. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) permits retrospective motion and strain estimation, but its changing image intensities and boundaries do not explicitly label myocardial material points. Regional interpretation therefore depends on the estimator's assumptions and on what its validation actually measures.

This critical narrative review compares feature tracking, registration, biomechanical and learning-based methods by their inputs, representations, priors and supervision. It separates evidence from known-motion tests, tagging or displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE), repeatability, tissue characterisation and clinical outcomes. Selected paired studies support circumferential-strain agreement at whole-slice, global and segmental scales under matched definitions. DENSE-supervised learning improved end-systolic cine--DENSE agreement, with residual individual differences and no external-dataset validation. Radial measurements are particularly sensitive to product and definition. A prescribed-scar evaluation of DeepStrain on MRXCAT2.0 reported component-specific errors averaged over whole slices; those results do not quantify recovery within the scar. Clinical discrimination and prognosis add useful evidence, but do not establish material-motion accuracy.

A defensible regional claim should specify the acquisition, estimator, population, spatial scale, strain definition, reference and tolerance. Controlled known-motion tests and aligned material-sensitive in-vivo comparisons can probe abnormality magnitude, location, extent and timing; downstream studies address clinical relevance. Input information, representation, estimation and reference uncertainty can contribute jointly to error. Matching the claim to these complementary tests is more informative than treating any single agreement measure as general validation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cardiovascular magnetic resonance; cine CMR; myocardial motion; myocardial strain; regional dysfunction; feature tracking; image registration; validation.

\section*{Plain English summary}
The heart can contain a small area that moves abnormally even when its overall pumping measure looks normal. Standard cine CMR provides moving pictures of the heart and is available in routine care. Computer methods can use these pictures to estimate motion and strain, a measure of deformation. An ordinary cine image, however, does not label each piece of heart muscle, so different methods can produce different internal motion while matching the same heart borders.

We reviewed how cine-based methods work and how they have been tested. Some studies show useful agreement with specialised CMR methods that are more sensitive to tissue motion. Other studies show important differences between software products, strain directions and heart regions. A good-looking contour, a repeatable number, an association with scar and a prediction of outcome are all valuable findings, and each of them answers a different question.

Regional measurements should therefore be judged for the exact property they are meant to report: the severity, position, extent or timing of an abnormality. Tests with known motion should be combined with carefully registered comparisons to specialised scans in people. The method, scan settings, strain definition and acceptable error should be stated in advance. This approach can identify where routine cine CMR gives reliable regional information and where the result should remain qualified.
