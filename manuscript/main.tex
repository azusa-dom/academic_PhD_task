\begin{abstract}
Regional myocardial dysfunction can be clinically important when global ventricular function is preserved. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) permits retrospective motion and strain estimation, but cine images do not explicitly identify myocardial material points, so their correspondence across frames must be inferred. Regional interpretation therefore depends on how an estimator infers that correspondence and on whether it has been validated against a target that matches the intended claim. This critical narrative review examines both dependencies. Feature-tracking, registration-based, biomechanical and learning-based estimators rely on different priors and supervision, yet many of the method papers reviewed are evaluated mainly by mask overlap or surface distance, which cannot distinguish material mappings that share the same contours, or by sparse landmarks, which constrain correspondence at only a few points. Such evidence can support volumes and contour-derived global strain but not finer regional claims. Sorted by validation target, many apparently conflicting results answer different questions. Studies pairing cine with tagging or displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE) support selected circumferential-strain endpoints, most consistently at global and mid-ventricular whole-slice level when strain definitions are matched. Agreement nevertheless varied with strain component, spatial scale and software product and was least consistent for radial strain. One DENSE-supervised network improved end-systolic agreement with DENSE over conventional feature tracking in internal testing, although individual differences remained wide. Direct tests of focal magnitude, location, extent and timing remain sparse, and scar-discrimination and prognostic studies address clinical relevance but do not by themselves establish material-motion accuracy. A regional claim should therefore specify the acquisition, estimator, population, spatial unit, strain definition and reference state, time point, comparator and a prespecified tolerance. Known-motion tests with prescribed focal abnormalities, paired in-vivo comparisons with tagging or DENSE, and repeatability studies address different parts of that claim, and no single agreement measure validates an estimator for every claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cardiovascular magnetic resonance; cine CMR; myocardial motion; myocardial strain; regional dysfunction; feature tracking; image registration; validation.

\section*{Plain English summary}
The heart can contain a small area that moves abnormally even when its overall pumping measure looks normal. Standard cine CMR provides moving pictures of the heart and is available in routine care. Computer methods can use these pictures to estimate motion and strain, a measure of deformation. An ordinary cine image, however, does not label each piece of heart muscle, so different methods can produce different internal motion while matching the same heart borders.

We reviewed how cine-based methods work and how they have been tested. Some studies show useful agreement with specialised CMR methods that are more sensitive to tissue motion. Other studies show important differences between software products, strain directions and heart regions. A good-looking contour, a repeatable number, an association with scar and a prediction of outcome are all valuable findings, and each of them answers a different question.

Regional measurements should therefore be judged for the exact property they are meant to report: the severity, position, extent or timing of an abnormality. Tests with known motion should be combined with carefully registered comparisons to specialised scans in people. The method, scan settings, strain definition and acceptable error should be stated in advance. This approach can identify where routine cine CMR gives reliable regional information and where the result should remain qualified.
