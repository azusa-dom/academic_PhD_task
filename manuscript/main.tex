\begin{abstract}
Regional myocardial dysfunction can be clinically important even when global ventricular function is preserved. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) permits retrospective motion and strain estimation, but cine images do not explicitly identify myocardial material points, so their correspondence across frames must be inferred. Regional interpretation therefore depends on how an estimator infers that correspondence, how strain and its spatial and temporal summaries are defined, and whether the estimator has been validated against a target that matches the intended claim. This critical narrative review examines these dependencies.

Feature-tracking, registration-based, biomechanical and learning-based estimators rely on different priors and supervision. Among the selected cardiac-specific method papers reviewed, reported validation often centres on mask overlap, surface distance or sparse landmarks. These endpoints assess geometry or correspondence at sampled locations, but cannot by themselves identify dense material mappings. Accurate boundaries can support volumes and, under an explicitly contour-based definition, some global strain measures, but they do not by themselves validate dense regional material motion or strain.

When evidence is organised by validation target, apparently conflicting findings often answer different questions. Studies pairing cine with tagging or displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE) support selected circumferential-strain endpoints, most consistently at global and mid-ventricular whole-slice scales when strain definitions are matched. Agreement nevertheless varies with strain component, spatial scale and software product, and radial strain is the least consistent component across the paired studies reviewed. One DENSE-supervised network improved end-systolic cine--DENSE agreement over its tested conventional feature-tracking pipeline in internal testing, although residual limits of agreement constrained individual-level inference.

Direct tests of focal magnitude, location, extent and timing remain sparse, and scar-discrimination and prognostic studies address biological or clinical relevance but do not by themselves establish material-motion accuracy. A regional accuracy claim should therefore specify the acquisition, estimator, population, spatial unit, strain definition and reference state, time point, comparator and a prespecified tolerance. Known-motion tests with prescribed focal abnormalities, paired in-vivo comparisons with tagging or DENSE, and repeatability studies address complementary parts of such a claim; no single agreement measure validates an estimator for every claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cardiovascular magnetic resonance; cine CMR; myocardial motion; myocardial strain; regional dysfunction; feature tracking; image registration; validation.

\section*{Plain English summary}
The heart can contain a small area that moves abnormally even when its overall pumping measure looks normal. Standard cine CMR provides moving pictures of the heart and is available in routine care. Computer methods can use these pictures to estimate motion and strain, a measure of deformation. An ordinary cine image, however, does not label each piece of heart muscle, so different methods can produce different internal motion while matching the same heart borders.

We reviewed how cine-based methods work and how they have been tested. Some studies show useful agreement with specialised CMR methods that are more sensitive to tissue motion. Other studies show important differences between software products, strain directions and heart regions. A good-looking contour, a repeatable number, an association with scar and a prediction of outcome are all valuable findings, and each of them answers a different question.

Regional measurements should therefore be judged for the exact property they are meant to report: the severity, position, extent or timing of an abnormality. Tests with known motion should be combined with carefully registered comparisons to specialised scans in people. The method, scan settings, strain definition and acceptable error should be stated in advance. This approach can identify where routine cine CMR gives reliable regional information and where the result should remain qualified.
