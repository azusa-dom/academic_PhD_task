\begin{abstract}
Regional myocardial dysfunction may be clinically important even when global ventricular measures remain preserved. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) is widely available and supports retrospective motion and strain estimation, but it records changing image intensity and anatomy rather than explicit labels for myocardial material points. The central question is therefore not whether motion can be estimated, but which regional attributes are recoverable from a specified cine acquisition, estimator and measurement definition, and what evidence supports that claim.

This critical narrative review synthesises feature-tracking, deformable-registration, biomechanical and learning-based approaches through a common causal chain: image formation and sampling; segmentation or correspondence estimation; regularisation and representation; strain calculation and aggregation; and validation. Evidence from numerical phantoms, tagging, displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE), scan--rescan studies, late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) and clinical outcomes is compared by the claim each target can support. STRAUS, the Stanford Cardiac Performance MRI repository, MRXCAT/MRXCAT2.0 and the Cardiac Motion Analysis Challenge are examined as complementary rather than interchangeable validation resources. The literature contains credible positive results: cine-derived circumferential strain can agree with DENSE or tagging in bounded settings, and DENSE-supervised learning has improved segmental agreement over conventional feature tracking. Agreement is generally stronger for global than regional quantities; radial strain is particularly method-dependent; the one independent test of a learning-based estimator against a prescribed focal scar preserved circumferential strain while underestimating radial strain; and evidence for focal boundary, transmural extent and peak-time recovery remains sparse in the studies reviewed.

These findings are reconciled by treating regional fidelity as attribute- and use-specific. Magnitude, location, spatial extent and timing may fail differently, and the same contours can conceal different intramyocardial correspondences. Smooth or low-dimensional representations should be tested for capacity rather than presumed to erase focal abnormalities. A defensible validation programme therefore separates representation capacity, estimation error and input sufficiency; combines known-motion tests with registered material-sensitive in-vivo comparisons; and pre-specifies estimands, tolerances and subgroups. Routine cine CMR can support useful regional measurements, but claims should remain tied to the method, acquisition, population, spatial scale, reference and decision for which they were demonstrated.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cardiovascular magnetic resonance; cine CMR; myocardial motion; myocardial strain; regional dysfunction; feature tracking; image registration; validation.

\section*{Plain English summary}
The heart can contain a small area that moves abnormally even when its overall pumping measure looks normal. Standard cine CMR provides moving pictures of the heart and is available in routine care. Computer methods can use these pictures to estimate motion and strain, a measure of deformation. However, an ordinary cine image does not label each piece of heart muscle. Different methods can therefore produce different internal motion while matching the same heart borders.

We reviewed how cine-based methods work and how they have been tested. Some studies show useful agreement with specialised CMR methods that are more sensitive to tissue motion. Other studies show important differences between software products, strain directions and heart regions. A good-looking contour, a repeatable number, an association with scar, and a prediction of outcome are valuable findings, but they do not prove the same thing.

Regional measurements should be judged for the exact property they are meant to report: the size, position, extent or timing of an abnormality. Tests with known motion should be combined with carefully registered comparisons to specialised scans in people. The method, scan settings, strain definition and acceptable error should be stated in advance. This approach can identify where routine cine CMR gives reliable regional information and where the result should remain qualified.
