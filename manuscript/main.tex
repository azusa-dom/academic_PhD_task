\begin{abstract}
Regional myocardial dysfunction can be clinically important despite preserved global ventricular function. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) permits retrospective motion and strain estimation, but cine images do not explicitly identify myocardial material points across frames. Regional interpretation therefore depends on the estimator's assumptions and on whether the validation target matches the intended claim.

This critical narrative review compares feature-tracking, registration-based, biomechanical and learning-based methods by their inputs, representations, priors and supervision. It distinguishes evidence from known-motion tests, comparisons with tagging or displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE), repeatability studies, tissue-characterisation analyses and clinical-outcome studies. Among the paired studies reviewed, cine-derived circumferential strain showed agreement with tagging or DENSE at whole-slice, global and segmental scales when definitions were matched; direct tests of focal magnitude, location, extent and timing remain sparse. DENSE-supervised learning improved end-systolic cine--DENSE agreement relative to conventional feature tracking (global intraclass correlation with DENSE 0.87 versus 0.72), although individual differences remained and external-dataset validation was absent. Radial-strain estimates are particularly sensitive to software product and strain definition. In a prescribed-scar MRXCAT2.0 evaluation of DeepStrain, component-specific errors were averaged over whole slices and therefore did not quantify recovery within the scar. Clinical discrimination and prognosis provide evidence of clinical relevance but do not, by themselves, establish material-motion accuracy.

A defensible regional claim should therefore specify the acquisition, estimator, population, spatial scale, strain definition, reference and a prespecified tolerance. Controlled known-motion tests and aligned material-sensitive in-vivo comparisons can probe abnormality magnitude, location, extent and timing; downstream studies address clinical relevance. Input information, representation, estimation and reference uncertainty can contribute jointly to error, so each claim is best supported by the complementary tests that address it; no single agreement measure serves as general validation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cardiovascular magnetic resonance; cine CMR; myocardial motion; myocardial strain; regional dysfunction; feature tracking; image registration; validation.

\section*{Plain English summary}
The heart can contain a small area that moves abnormally even when its overall pumping measure looks normal. Standard cine CMR provides moving pictures of the heart and is available in routine care. Computer methods can use these pictures to estimate motion and strain, a measure of deformation. An ordinary cine image, however, does not label each piece of heart muscle, so different methods can produce different internal motion while matching the same heart borders.

We reviewed how cine-based methods work and how they have been tested. Some studies show useful agreement with specialised CMR methods that are more sensitive to tissue motion. Other studies show important differences between software products, strain directions and heart regions. A good-looking contour, a repeatable number, an association with scar and a prediction of outcome are all valuable findings, and each of them answers a different question.

Regional measurements should therefore be judged for the exact property they are meant to report: the severity, position, extent or timing of an abnormality. Tests with known motion should be combined with carefully registered comparisons to specialised scans in people. The method, scan settings, strain definition and acceptable error should be stated in advance. This approach can identify where routine cine CMR gives reliable regional information and where the result should remain qualified.
