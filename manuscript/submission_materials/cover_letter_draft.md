Dear Editors of the *Journal of Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance*,

Please consider the Review article, “From routine cine CMR to regional myocardial dysfunction: Fidelity, validation and interpretation of motion and strain estimates,” by Ziying Huo.

Regional motion and strain estimates are increasingly produced from routine cine CMR, yet studies often validate different links in the measurement chain. Mask overlap, scan–rescan precision, agreement with tagging or DENSE, association with late gadolinium enhancement, and outcome prediction are all valuable, but they do not support identical claims. This review brings those literatures together around a practical question: for a specified cine acquisition, estimator and strain definition, which attributes of a regional abnormality—magnitude, location, spatial extent and timing—are recovered within a tolerance appropriate to the intended use?

The article is intended for JCMR’s combined clinical and technical readership. It compares feature tracking, deformable registration, biomechanical approaches and learning-based methods by their inputs, assumptions, estimands, populations and validation targets. It also reviews STRAUS, the Stanford Cardiac Performance MRI repository, CMAC and MRXCAT/MRXCAT2.0 as complementary resources for known-motion, sparse-correspondence and paired material-sensitive evaluation. It synthesises positive and contradictory findings rather than presuming that cine-derived regional measurement succeeds or fails as a class. For technical readers it also traces the estimator literature from free-form deformation and early feature tracking to learned registration, cardiac-specific, physics-informed and DENSE-supervised designs, with a year-ordered method table in the supplement, and it reports the one independent prescribed-focal test retrieved together with its limits.

The focus differs from recent broad reviews of myocardial strain and clinical practice, reviews of deep-learning wall-motion analysis across modalities, and reviews of CMR/CT strain. The submitted review specifically examines regional or focal fidelity from routine cine CMR and the strength of inference supported by each validation target. It does not claim to be the first review of myocardial strain or cardiac motion.
