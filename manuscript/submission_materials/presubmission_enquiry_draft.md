## Proposed title

**From routine cine CMR to regional myocardial dysfunction: Fidelity, validation and interpretation of motion and strain estimates**

## Rationale and relevance to JCMR

Routine cine CMR is widely available, and feature tracking, registration, biomechanical and learning-based approaches can produce regional motion or strain retrospectively. The resulting literature is difficult to interpret because different studies validate different links in the measurement chain. A method may reproduce contours, agree globally with another product, be repeatable, discriminate scar or predict outcome without each result establishing the same level of regional material-motion fidelity. Conversely, paired tagging/DENSE studies and known-motion experiments provide meaningful positive evidence in bounded settings and should not be obscured by a generic critique of cine estimation.

The proposed Review offers a clinically oriented technical synthesis centred on a single question: for a specified acquisition, estimator and measurement definition, which attributes of a focal or regional abnormality—magnitude, location, extent and timing—are recoverable at an error tolerance appropriate to the intended use? It also treats reconstruction as part of the input contract: primary CineVN, REGAIN, DENT and model-based-DLR comparisons show useful image, volume or global/peak-strain results, but do not by themselves test focal location or extent. This framing is directly relevant to JCMR readers developing, validating or interpreting cine-derived deformation measures.

## Difference from adjacent recent reviews

Recent reviews have provided broad accounts of myocardial strain theory and practice, surveyed deep learning for cardiac wall-motion analysis across imaging modalities, or reviewed strain assessment with CMR and CT. This article does not attempt another modality-wide catalogue. Its distinct focus is the causal path from routine cine image information to inferred myocardial correspondence, strain definition and regional claim, followed by a comparison of what known-motion simulation, tagging/DENSE, repeatability, LGE and outcome studies can each establish. It explicitly reconciles successful and contradictory regional validations and does not claim priority as the first strain or motion review.

## Extended abstract

Regional myocardial dysfunction may be clinically important even when global ventricular measures remain preserved. Routine cine CMR supports retrospective motion and strain estimation but records changing anatomy and image intensity rather than explicit labels for myocardial material points. The central question is therefore which regional attributes are recoverable from a specified cine acquisition, estimator and measurement definition, and what evidence supports that claim.

This critical narrative review compares feature tracking, deformable registration, biomechanical and learning-based approaches through a common causal chain: image formation and sampling; segmentation or correspondence estimation; regularisation and representation; strain calculation and aggregation; and validation. Evidence from numerical phantoms, tagging, DENSE, scan–rescan studies, LGE and outcomes is organised by the claim each target can support. STRAUS, Stanford, CMAC and MRXCAT/MRXCAT2.0 are assessed as complementary validation resources. The literature contains credible positive results: cine-derived circumferential strain can agree with material-sensitive CMR in bounded settings, and DENSE-supervised StrainNet showed higher end-systolic segmental circumferential-strain agreement than conventional feature tracking in its independent test cohort. Its reported pixelwise displacement error was a separate evaluation that used DENSE-magnitude contours as input, not cine contours. Persistent qualifications remain: agreement is generally stronger globally than regionally; radial strain is method-dependent; and direct focal-boundary, extent and timing errors are uncommon in the studies reviewed.

The findings are reconciled by treating regional fidelity as attribute- and use-specific. Magnitude, location, extent and timing can fail differently, while identical contours can conceal different intramyocardial mappings. Smooth or low-dimensional representations should be challenged for capacity rather than presumed to erase abnormalities. A defensible programme separates representation capacity, estimation and input sufficiency; combines known-motion tests with registered material-sensitive in-vivo comparisons; and pre-specifies estimands, tolerances and subgroups. Routine cine CMR can support useful regional measurements, but claims should remain tied to the method, acquisition, population, spatial scale, reference and decision in which they were demonstrated.
