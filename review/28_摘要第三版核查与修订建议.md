## 修订稿（377 词，改动只为解决上述问题和压缩字数）

Regional myocardial dysfunction can be clinically important even when global ventricular function is preserved. Routine cine cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) permits retrospective motion and strain estimation, but cine images do not explicitly identify myocardial material points, so their correspondence across frames must be inferred. Regional interpretation therefore depends on how an estimator infers that correspondence, how strain and its spatial and temporal summaries are defined, and whether validation used a target that matches the intended claim. This critical narrative review examines these dependencies.

Feature-tracking, registration-based, biomechanical and learning-based estimators rely on different priors and supervision. Because cine intensity varies little within the wall, the estimate there is likely constrained more by the prior than by the image. Among the selected cardiac-specific method papers reviewed, validation often centres on mask overlap, surface distance or sparse landmarks. Accurate boundaries can support volumes and, under an explicitly contour-based definition, some global strain measures, but they do not validate dense regional motion or strain: identical masks admit different interior motion, and strain is a spatial derivative of displacement.

Organised by validation target, apparently conflicting findings often answer different questions. Paired comparisons with tagging or displacement encoding with stimulated echoes (DENSE) support circumferential strain most consistently at global and mid-ventricular whole-slice scales under matched definitions; agreement varies with component, scale and software, and radial strain is least consistent. One DENSE-supervised network improved end-systolic cine–DENSE agreement over its tested feature-tracking pipeline in internal testing, with residual limits of agreement that constrain individual-level inference.

Direct tests of focal magnitude, location, extent and timing remain sparse: location has been examined only through segment-level correspondence with late gadolinium enhancement, focal extent was not directly tested in any study reviewed, and the two direct timing comparisons found poor cine–DENSE agreement for a dyssynchrony index and moderate cine–tagging agreement for septal and lateral time to peak. Scar discrimination and prognosis address clinical relevance rather than material-motion accuracy, and repeatability bounds precision without bounding bias. A regional accuracy claim should therefore specify acquisition, estimator, population, spatial unit, strain definition, myocardial layer, reference state, time point, comparator and a prespecified tolerance. Known-motion tests with prescribed focal abnormalities, paired in-vivo comparisons and repeatability studies address complementary parts of such a claim; no single agreement measure validates an estimator for every claim.
